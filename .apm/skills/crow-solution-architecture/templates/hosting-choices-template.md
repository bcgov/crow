# Hosting and delivery choices

Use these tables as a worksheet inside Section 6 of the canonical solution
architecture. Replace every placeholder with evidence for the current
solution. Do not assume one option is universally preferred.

## Hosting options

| Option | Fit and candidate benefits | Candidate drawbacks or constraints | Workload and data considerations | Geographic considerations | Operational ownership | Evidence and status |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| B.C. government data centre (VM-STMS) | Well established Ops model, high performance CPUs available, large VMs (CPU/memory) available, high I/O bandwidth, high and unmetered network bandwidth, IPS/IDS coverage, suitable for database workloads | No self-provisioning of VMs, firewall and load balancer changes can be slow (several days), high availability requires multiple VMs, Zero Trust is hard to implement | Up to Protected C data, Canadian data residency, NetBackup with offsite backup built in | Kamloops, Calgary, and Vancouver | App Management Infrastructure teams | Confirmed, available |
| B.C. government data centre (VM-SDN) | Somewhat new Ops model, high performance CPUs available, large VMs (CPU/memory) available, high I/O bandwidth, high and unmetered network bandwidth, IPS/IDS coverage, Zero Trust compatible, instant changes to firewall and load balancer, suitable for database workloads | No self-provisioning of VMs, tagging of network traffic not well understood yet by most teams, high availability requires multiple VMs | Up to Protected C data, Canadian data residency, NetBackup with offsite backup built in | Kamloops and Calgary | App Management Infrastructure teams | Confirmed, available |
| Private-cloud OpenShift (Silver/Gold) | Somewhat mature Ops model, self-serve provisioning, rapid scaling, and high availability, unmetered network bandwidth, IPS/IDS coverage, default-allow network policy (simple), integrated monitoring and logging | Learning curve for container orchestration and OpenShift operations, limited CPU and memory resources, shared network and disk I/O resources, default-allow network policy (insecure), not Zero Trust compatible, requires HA application architecture, no built-in local or offsite backups, database workloads not recommended | Up to Protected B data, Canadian data residency | Kamloops (Silver, Gold) and Calgary (Gold DR) | Product teams | Confirmed, available |
| Private-cloud OpenShift (Emerald) | Somewhat mature Ops model, self-serve provisioning, rapid scaling, and high availability, unmetered network bandwidth, IPS/IDS coverage, SDN, default-deny network policy (secure), Zero Trust compatible, integrated monitoring and logging, suitable for small database workloads, GPUs for AI workloads available soon | Learning curve for container orchestration, SDN, and OpenShift operations, limited CPU and memory resources, shared network and disk I/O resources, default-deny network policy (complex), requires HA application architecture, no built-in local or offsite backups | Up to Protected C data, Canadian data residency, SDN enables access to Protected C data on STMS database servers | Kamloops only | Product teams | Confirmed, available |
| Public-cloud landing zone (AWS or Azure) | Somewhat mature Ops model, self-serve provisioning, rapid scaling, and high availability, ExpressRoute available, IPS/IDS coverage as landing zone traffic is routed through B.C. government data centre, Zero Trust compatible, managed databases available, many services available that are unfeasible in B.C. Government data centers and private-cloud OpenShift, massive resources available, mature automation capabilities | Everything is metered (network, CPU, disk I/O), higher latency to B.C. government data centre and private-cloud OpenShift, learning curve for overwhelming amount of available services and options | Up to Protected B data, Canadian data residency for most services | Many geographic regions available | Product team | Confirmed, available |

Note: Cost considerations have been omitted from this template as those are too unique for each option. Make sure to evaluate the total cost based on the specific requirements, constraints, and expected lifetime of the solution.

Data-centre hosting is a first-class option. It does not require a modernization justification or exit plan merely because it uses data-centre infrastructure. Consider it directly for mission-critical workloads, specialized infrastructure, predictable capacity, or high CPU, bandwidth, or I/O requirements.

## Delivery options

| Option | Fit and candidate benefits | Candidate drawbacks or constraints | Release, scaling, and recovery | Operational ownership | Evidence and status |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Virtual machines | Broad operating-system and vendor-software compatibility; strong workload isolation; predictable CPU, memory, network, and storage performance; suitable for specialized software and established infrastructure operations | Operating-system patching, hardening, configuration drift, capacity planning, and base-image lifecycle require active management; horizontal scaling and high availability require multiple instances and application support | Use automated, repeatable deployments where practical. Define rollback, capacity expansion, load-balancing, backup, restore, and multi-instance recovery procedures. Recovery objectives depend on the application architecture and selected hosting option. | Infrastructure teams manage the compute, network, backup platform, and approved base images. Product or application-management teams own application deployment, configuration, observability, vulnerability remediation, and recovery testing. | Confirmed delivery option; select when compatibility, isolation, performance, vendor, or operational requirements favour VMs |
| Containers | Immutable application packaging; consistent promotion between environments; efficient resource use; self-service deployment; rapid horizontal scaling and platform-supported scheduling, health checks, and restart | Requires container, orchestration, image, network-policy, secrets, and persistent-storage expertise; applications must support distributed operation and ephemeral instances; shared platform limits can constrain CPU, memory, network, and disk I/O | Promote versioned images through environments and retain a tested rollback image. Configure health probes, resource requests and limits, replica counts, disruption budgets, autoscaling where justified, and application-aware backup and restore for persistent data. | Platform teams operate the cluster and shared services. Product teams own images, deployments, application network policies, resource configuration, observability, vulnerability remediation, data protection, and recovery testing. | Confirmed delivery option on supported OpenShift platforms; solution fit remains subject to workload, data, resilience, and team-capability evidence |
| Platform-native or serverless deployment | Reduces infrastructure administration; provides rapid provisioning, managed scaling, and access to provider-native integration, security, data, and observability services | Runtime and service constraints can require application redesign; distributed-service complexity, metered consumption, quotas, service availability, observability, data location, and provider coupling require evaluation | Use infrastructure as code and automated promotion. Define versioning, rollback or traffic-shifting, concurrency and scaling limits, retry and idempotency behaviour, service backups, regional recovery, and a tested continuity approach for every stateful dependency. | The provider operates the managed platform. Product teams remain accountable for service configuration, identity and access, application security, cost controls, observability, data protection, dependency continuity, and recovery testing. | Confirmed capability in supported public-cloud landing zones; individual services require security, privacy, residency, operational, and financial assessment |
| Vendor or managed deployment | Can provide a mature supported product, specialized capability, predictable service levels, and reduced custom development or platform-operation effort | May limit integration, customization, observability, deployment control, data access, and recovery testing; introduces supplier, contract, licensing, data-portability, residency, and exit dependencies | Document vendor release and maintenance controls, rollback commitments, capacity limits, backup and restore responsibilities, tested continuity arrangements, service-level remedies, data export, and transition or termination procedures. | The contract must assign vendor and ministry responsibilities for operations, access, monitoring, incident response, vulnerability remediation, backup, recovery, privacy, records management, and service transition. The ministry retains accountability for the service and its information. | Available where procurement, architecture, security, privacy, records, service-level, and exit requirements are confirmed; product-specific evidence required |

Virtual machines are a first-class delivery option when their compatibility, performance, isolation, specialized-software, or operational characteristics best satisfy the requirements. Do not require a modernization path solely because virtual machines are selected.
